The desk playbook

  1. Drive it before anyone says a number

    Do not sit down at a desk first. While you are at the car, check the window sticker for GT Premium — ventilated fronts, heated rears, heated wheel, head-up display. Vented seats have a fan icon on the seat switch; the HUD shows the moment it starts. Confirm two fobs, look at tire date codes and brake wear, and ask for service records.

  2. Make them present first

    Never counter into silence. Ask for their best number now that you have driven it and let them talk. Anything they say below $28,123 is movement you did not have to spend anything for.

    “We drove it, we like it. What is your best out-the-door on it today?”

  3. Anchor $26,500 out the door

    You already told Ayres that $27,600 was a great start, so a lower number is the continuation of that conversation, not a reversal. Give the reason out loud so it lands as analysis rather than haggling: this car is out of factory warranty at 33,000 miles, and the certified 2023 you are seeing later carries Mazda coverage to roughly 2030.

    “This one is out of warranty. The certified 2023 we are driving later is covered to 2030. On this car we are at $26,500 out the door.”

  4. Say you have more cars to drive, because you do

    Puyallup is a real second stop with two real cars and written numbers. This is the strongest sentence you will say all morning and it has the advantage of being completely true. Then stop talking.

    “We have two more to drive today. Give us your best and we will let you know either way this afternoon.”

  5. Trade stays in your pocket

    Do not raise a trade until the out-the-door number is agreed in writing. Introduced early, it lets them shuffle money between price and trade allowance and show you a worse deal that looks better. One number at a time.

  6. Be willing to actually leave

    Walking out and coming back is a normal move and it costs nothing here. The car has been listed 48 days and it will still be there this afternoon. Expect to land somewhere around $27,000–27,400; $27,600 is already banked. And if they dig in, $28,123 is the lowest total out-the-door on the whole board — about $3,755 under CarMax measured all-in — so it is worth signing.

  7. Read every line before you sign

    The buyer's order must match: selling price, tax, license at actuals with overage refunded, $200 doc, zero add-ons. Their sheets were clean twice, so a new line at signing is deliberate. Decline paint protection, etch, nitrogen, appearance packages. Extended warranty is a separate decision for another day, never under desk pressure.

Financing: the BECU gap

Maci has a BECU preapproval but no visible rate. That is a real gap to close before signing, because without an APR there is nothing to compare the dealer's offer against.

Where the rate usually hides

Credit-union preapprovals commonly show an approved amount up front and set the rate at final underwriting, keyed to term, model year, and credit tier. Check, in order: the loan or preapproval section inside becu.org online banking, then the approval email or PDF itself, then the member line. Ask specifically for APR, term, expiration date, any mileage or model-year restriction, and whether funding is a draft you bring or a dealer-pay arrangement. A 2021 with 33k miles finances easily, but some tiers price older model years differently than a 2023, which matters if the choice shifts to the certified car.

How to play it at the desk today

Keep price and money completely separate. Agree the out-the-door number first and get it written, then handle financing. That stance is already established with Kirkland: the price is not contingent on using their lender, in writing. If the BECU rate is still unknown when the paperwork starts, it is fine to let the dealer run their bank and show you a number, purely as a comparison. What must not happen is a rate concession getting traded for a price concession, or a lower payment being used to paper over a higher total.

If the BECU rate turns out to be unavailable today and the deal is otherwise good, signing with dealer financing and refinancing through BECU later is a normal path, as long as there is no prepayment penalty. Confirm that before agreeing to it.
