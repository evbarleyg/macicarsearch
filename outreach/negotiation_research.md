# Used CX-5 by email: negotiation + WA/OR findings memo
Prepared 2026-08-11 (Tue) for a Seattle buyer emailing 7 dealers (5 WA, 2 OR) about specific used 2021-2023 Mazda CX-5s (~$25-28k), credit-union pre-approval in hand, no trade, goal = written itemized OTD below advertised price before any call, visit 3 on Sat 8/15.

Legend: [V] = verified on an official/primary or top-tier consumer source; [P] = practitioner consensus (Edmunds/CR/NerdWallet/CarEdge/McParland etc.), not hard data; [U] = could not fully verify -- treat as estimate.

---

## A. Cold email outreach -- what actually gets a real number back

- **Address a named person in the internet department (Internet Sales Manager / BDC), or go one level up to the Used Car Manager / GSM if the internet rep can only "set an appointment."** Edmunds says to email/text the internet sales manager and include the stock #; CarEdge argues for emailing the sales manager/GM directly because they hold pricing authority and internet reps are measured on appointments, not price. Dealer-side metrics confirm BDC/internet teams are graded on appointment-set and show rates, which is why many replies dodge price and push a visit. [P] -- https://www.edmunds.com/car-buying/how-to-get-an-internet-price-quote-for-a-car.html ; https://caredge.com/guides/negotiate-a-car-by-email ; https://maritz.com/resources/dealership-internet-sales-metric-faq/
- **Subject line: make it scannable and specific to one unit** -- e.g., "OTD request -- 2022 CX-5 Premium, Stk #12345 -- buying this week." Templates from CarEdge/AutoCheatSheet-style guides all lead with year/model/trim + "price/OTD request"; including stock # or VIN removes the "let me look it up / is it still here" stall. [P] -- https://caredge.com/guides/email-templates-to-buy-a-car-online ; https://www.edmunds.com/car-buying/how-to-get-an-internet-price-quote-for-a-car.html
- **Length: 5-8 sentences. One car, one ask.** Every credible template is short: who you are + zip, the exact unit, proof you are real (timeline, financing arranged, no trade), the single ask (itemized OTD in writing), and how you want to be contacted. Long emails with multiple questions get routed to "call the customer." [P] -- https://caredge.com/guides/email-templates-to-buy-a-car-online ; https://www.consumerreports.org/car-pricing-negotiation/how-to-negotiate-car-prices-by-email-or-phone/
- **Signal a near-term, low-friction buyer explicitly.** Phrases that recur in the templates dealers respond to: "ready to buy this week," "financing already arranged / pre-approved," "no trade," "can leave a deposit by card once we agree on numbers." CR's script: "I'm ready to buy right now, but I'm shopping around and I want your best out-the-door price." RealCarTips: tell them you are buying in the next couple of days. [P] -- https://www.consumerreports.org/car-pricing-negotiation/how-to-negotiate-car-prices-by-email-or-phone/ ; https://www.realcartips.com/usedcars/501-negotiate-used-car-price.shtml ; https://www.nerdwallet.com/auto-loans/learn/advantages-of-getting-pre-approved-for-a-car-loan
- **Ask for an itemized OTD to your Seattle zip (98xxx), not "best price."** Ask them to break out: selling price, doc fee, any dealer-installed items/packages, sales tax rate used, license/title/RTA estimate. McParland (professional buyer): "always get itemized quotes in writing and question extraneous fees"; CarEdge template: "best detailed out-the-door price breakdown including ... dealer and government fees." An "about $X" reply is not a quote -- ask again for the line items. [P] -- https://www.jalopnik.com/how-used-car-dealers-are-trying-to-game-the-online-pric-1837030397/ ; https://caredge.com/guides/email-templates-to-buy-a-car-online
- **Batching: yes, contact all 7 the same morning, and say so plainly (without theatrics).** Edmunds: get at least three quotes to see the real market; CarEdge template: "I will be contacting several dealers within a 75-100 mile radius"; CR: "I'm shopping around." For used cars each unit is unique, so do NOT cc dealers on one blast (that new-car tactic from MoneyUnder30 does not translate); send individual emails that each reference that dealer's specific stock #, and mention you are comparing "a short list of similar CX-5s at other stores this week." [P] -- https://www.edmunds.com/car-buying/how-to-get-an-internet-price-quote-for-a-car.html ; https://caredge.com/guides/email-templates-to-buy-a-car-online ; https://www.moneyunder30.com/get-the-best-deal-on-a-new-car/
- **Send day/time: weekday business hours, ideally Tue-Thu morning.** Dealer-side guidance stresses "speed to lead" (respond inside ~20-30 min) and internet staff are at desks on weekday mornings; weekends they are pulled to the floor. Edmunds also notes weekdays are slower for sales staff. I found no controlled data on buyer-side send time -- treat as reasonable practice, not proven. [P/U] -- https://maritz.com/resources/dealership-internet-sales-metric-faq/ ; https://www.edmunds.com/car-buying/best-time-to-buy-a-used-car.html
- **Expected response: roughly half to two-thirds will reply; fewer will send a true itemized OTD on the first ask.** MoneyUnder30's multi-dealer email method reports ~6-7 of 10 dealers writing back; McParland says some stores still refuse written quotes and you "just have to grind it out" and move on to stores that will. Plan on 7 sent -> ~4-5 replies -> ~3 usable OTDs, which matches your "visit 3" plan. [P/U] -- https://www.moneyunder30.com/get-the-best-deal-on-a-new-car/ ; https://www.jalopnik.com/why-are-dealers-still-insisting-that-i-negotiate-in-per-1842791873/
- **Follow-ups: one nudge at ~24h, one final "deciding tomorrow" note; then stop.** Dealer CRMs will keep following *you* for weeks (industry guidance: "follow up for longer than you think is necessary"), so silence after two touches means "no discount available by email," not "they didn't see it." [P] -- https://maritz.com/resources/dealership-internet-sales-metric-faq/ ; https://caredge.com/guides/negotiate-a-car-by-email
- **What dealership-side people say makes them take an email lead seriously vs. ignore it** (r/askcarsales itself could not be fetched from this environment -- Reddit blocks the crawler -- so this is drawn from dealer-facing training material and consumer writers who summarize that community; flagging as [U] for direct attribution): serious = specific in-stock unit, local-ish zip, clear timeline, financing settled, reachable, polite, realistic; ignored = generic "what's your best price on a CX-5," offers 15-20% under market with no rationale, obvious 10-dealer form blasts, refusal to ever visit, and payment shoppers. McParland: an offer far from achievable makes the seller think "you have an unrealistic expectation" and they disengage; internet departments' real KPI is appointments that show. -- https://www.jalopnik.com/why-are-dealers-refusing-to-negotiate-if-i-make-a-reall-1842917825/ ; https://maritz.com/resources/dealership-internet-sales-metric-faq/ ; https://askthemanager.com/2016/06/car-dealer-internet-sales-it-really-aint-all-that-hard/
- **Common mistakes to avoid:** (1) giving a phone number in the first email -- lead forms force it and the store will call to move you off email (CarEdge: withhold it; use the rep's direct email, or write "email only until we have numbers"); (2) lowballing with no comps; (3) discussing monthly payment, down payment, or "how will you be paying" before the OTD is fixed (CarEdge: "I'm not concerned with the monthly payment, I am only focused on the total out-the-door price"); (4) accepting a verbal/phone number without a written recap (Edmunds: ask them to email it); (5) negotiating in the showroom on visit day instead of arriving with the number already agreed (RealCarTips). ## B. Negotiation levers for USED cars at franchise dealers (2025-2026)

- **Realistic discount off the advertised internet price: usually $300-$1,000 (about 1-4%) on a market-priced 3-5-year-old compact SUV; $1,000-$2,000 only on aged or overpriced units; sometimes $0 on a fresh, well-priced one.** Franchise stores price used inventory to live market data (vAuto/KBB tools), so McParland's framing is the accurate one: "the used car market doesn't usually have a ton of margin for negotiation ... you are really shopping the best value over the biggest discount." Older "10-20% markup" rules of thumb (RealCarTips, AutoCheatSheet) predate market-based pricing and overstate today's room. Market context: Cox/KBB show used prices flat into summer 2026 (avg listing ~$27.0k, ~47 days' supply) -- a normal, not distressed, market. [P; the $ ranges are practitioner estimates, not audited data -- U] -- https://www.jalopnik.com/why-wont-dealers-negotiate-on-pre-owned-cars-1830174631/ ; https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/is-now-the-time-to-buy-sell-or-trade-in-a-used-car/ ; https://cartipsdaily.com/how-much-will-dealers-come-down-on-a-used-car
- **Days-on-lot and prior price drops are the single best predictor of flexibility.** Dealers floorplan used inventory and most run 60- or 90-day aging policies (wholesale/auction after that), so a unit at 45-90+ days with one or two price cuts already taken is where $1,000+ asks land; a unit listed <3 weeks ago at a "Great Deal" rating has little room. Check CarGurus/Cars.com "days on market" and price-history, and the Carfax listing date, for each of the 7 units before you write. [P] -- https://www.realcartips.com/cardealers/041-manaufacture-date-of-a-vehicle.shtml ; https://caredge.com/guides/how-smart-car-shoppers-buy-cars-in-2026-to-save-money ; https://cartipsdaily.com/how-much-will-dealers-come-down-on-a-used-car
- **Anchor with comps, including no-haggle sellers.** McParland: the way to move a used price "is not to just throw a number out there, but to find a more competitive bid on a similar car"; if you cannot find a cheaper comparable, the car is probably already priced right. Use 2-3 same-year/trim/mileage listings (other franchise stores, CarMax, Carvana -- whose prices are fixed and public) plus Edmunds/KBB dealer-retail values; Edmunds says aim between trade-in and dealer-retail. [P] -- https://www.jalopnik.com/why-are-dealers-refusing-to-negotiate-if-i-make-a-reall-1842917825/ ; https://www.edmunds.com/car-buying/negotiating-101.html ; https://www.nerdwallet.com/auto-loans/learn/negotiating-basics-buying-car
- **Round 1: let them bid first (ask for itemized OTD); Round 2: counter once with a justified, specific OTD number.** RealCarTips: "always start off by asking the seller what they really need to do the deal" and get the other side to name the first number; CarEdge likewise asks for the OTD breakdown before naming a price. Then counter with a precise figure tied to comps/days-on-lot (Edmunds: open below target but "in the ballpark"; NerdWallet: shrink increments if there is a round 3 -- $500, then $250). One counter, maybe one small final move; more rounds by email rarely produce more money on a used unit. [P] -- https://www.realcartips.com/usedcars/501-negotiate-used-car-price.shtml ; https://caredge.com/guides/negotiate-a-car-by-email ; https://www.nerdwallet.com/auto-loans/learn/negotiating-basics-buying-car
- **The conditional close is the strongest email lever on used cars:** "If you can do $X out-the-door as itemized, I will put a card deposit down today and come sign Saturday morning." CarEdge's email guide: to get a dealer to budge off a used-car internet price you usually must convey urgency -- i.e., say you'll put a deposit down now if they discount it; RealCarTips: tell them you're buying today; CarEdge's fallback line to a stonewalling store: "Pick a price reduction, let me know what it is, and I'll come and buy the car today." [P] -- https://caredge.com/guides/negotiate-a-car-by-email ; https://caredge.com/guides/email-templates-to-buy-a-car-online ; https://www.realcartips.com/usedcars/501-negotiate-used-car-price.shtml
- **Add-ons and fees -- what is legitimate in WA/OR and what to strike.** Legitimate: selling price, sales/use tax, actual state title/license/RTA fees, and a doc fee within the cap (WA <= $200 and must be disclosed as *negotiable*; OR <= $250 w/ integrator or $200 without, also negotiable). Strike or demand removal/offset: "reconditioning"/"certification"/"prep"/"inspection" fees, nitrogen, VIN etch, paint/fabric/theft "protection packages," "market adjustment," and in Oregon a "CAT recovery" line presented as a tax (Oregon DOR: dealers may pass their Corporate Activity Tax cost through, but it is not a tax on the buyer and must not be shown as a government tax -- treat it as negotiable price). Crucially, both states' advertising rules say the advertised price must already include everything except tax, license/registration and the doc fee: WA WAC 308-66-152(4)(k) bars "adding charges, costs, or items to the advertised price" other than buyer-ordered equipment, sales tax and license fees, and (4)(j) bars selling above the advertised price; Oregon OAR 137-020-0020(3)(c),(j) requires any advertised/quoted price to be the full "offering price" excluding only taxes, license/registration, DEQ and doc fee, and bans undisclosed "price packing." Quote these calmly if a mandatory package appears. [V] -- https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=308-66-152 ; https://www.atg.wa.gov/dealer-advertising ; https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_137-020-0020 ; https://www.oregon.gov/dor/programs/businesses/pages/corporate-activity-tax.aspx ; https://caredge.com/guides/understanding-car-dealer-fees ; https://www.edmunds.com/car-buying/what-fees-should-you-pay.html
- **Financing as a chip: "pre-approved, but open to your lender if the overall deal is right."** Dealers earn finance reserve (buy-rate markup typically capped ~2-3 points) plus a flat from the lender, so a deal they might finance is worth more to them than a "cash"/outside-check deal; McParland: being a cash buyer will not get you a better price. Keeping the door open can buy a few hundred on the front end; then in F&I only sign if their APR truly beats the credit union with no add-ons attached, and confirm no prepayment penalty (CarEdge line: "I would, assuming that you can beat the pre-approved rate that I have"). Do not reveal the CU rate until the OTD is locked. [P] -- https://www.nerdwallet.com/auto-loans/learn/dealers-profit-off-financing ; https://www.jalopnik.com/this-is-the-best-car-buying-advice-from-jalopniks-own-c-1851115993/ ; https://caredge.com/guides/negotiate-car-prices-cheat-sheet ; https://www.edmunds.com/car-buying/how-to-get-pre-approved-for-a-car-loan.html
- **Timing: month-end helps a little; unit age helps more.** Edmunds: last days of the month can matter if the store is short of a target, and weekdays get you more attention; used departments also track monthly units, but a mid-August Saturday (8/15) is not month-end, so lean on days-on-lot and competing quotes rather than the calendar. If a finalist stalls, "I can wait until the 29th-31st" is a credible, mild threat. [P] -- https://www.edmunds.com/car-buying/best-time-to-buy-a-used-car.html ; https://www.edmunds.com/car-buying/when-to-buy-your-next-car.html
- **One-counter discipline and walking away.** RealCarTips: negotiation "works best if you are easily able to walk away (or at least make the other party think you will)" -- which is exactly what 7 parallel units give you; NerdWallet/Edmunds: be ready to walk, and never re-open price in the showroom -- if the buyer's order does not match the emailed OTD line-for-line, leave. [P] -- https://www.realcartips.com/usedcars/501-negotiate-used-car-price.shtml ; https://www.nerdwallet.com/auto-loans/learn/negotiating-basics-buying-car ; https://www.consumerreports.org/car-pricing-negotiation/how-to-negotiate-car-prices-by-email-or-phone/
- **CPO angles (Mazda dealers only).** Mazda CPO adds a 12-mo/12k-mi limited warranty extension plus 7-yr/100k-mi powertrain from original in-service date, $0 deductible, 160-point inspection; that is worth real money on a 2021-2022 whose 36k bumper-to-bumper has lapsed, so compare CPO vs non-CPO units on *net* value, not sticker. Expect less price movement on CPO (certification cost is sunk), so ask instead for things with low dealer cost: strike the doc fee, include the second key/floor mats/full tank, fresh tires or brakes if the inspection sheet shows them near limits, and ask for the CPO inspection report and Carfax in the reply. At non-Mazda franchise stores, "certified" is a store program -- ask exactly what warranty backs it and decline any separate "certification fee." ## C. Washington / Oregon specifics (verified where marked)

- **WA doc fee cap: $200, and it must be disclosed in writing as negotiable.** RCW 46.70.180(2) makes it unlawful to charge a documentary service fee unless it is disclosed as negotiable, separately stated, capped at "an amount not to exceed $200 per vehicle sale or lease," and not represented as state-required; last amended 2022 c 182 (the $150 -> $200 change effective 7/1/2022); no later amendment appears in the RCW history as of this check. DOR adds that the doc fee is not subject to sales tax. [V] -- https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=46.70.180 ; https://dor.wa.gov/education/industry-guides/auto-dealers/miscellaneous ; https://www.atg.wa.gov/dealer-advertising
- **Correction to the model -- two things are off in "10.35% based on buyer's Seattle address":** (1) For a purchase from a *Washington dealer*, vehicle sales tax is sourced to the **seller's (dealer's) location**, not the buyer's address -- DOR: "Sales of motor vehicles ... sales tax is based on the seller's location even if the seller delivers the items to the customer." Buyer's-address sourcing applies to **use tax** (out-of-state/Oregon purchases and private sales). (2) The statewide additional motor-vehicle sales/use tax rose from **0.3% to 0.5% on Jan 1, 2026** (ESSB 5801). [V] -- https://dor.wa.gov/taxes-rates/sales-use-tax-rates/determine-location-my-sale ; https://dor.wa.gov/taxes-rates/other-taxes/motor-vehicle-salesuse-tax ; https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2025-26/Pdf/Bill%20Reports/Senate/5801-S.E%20SBR%20FBR%2025.pdf
- **Correct all-in vehicle rates for Q3 2026 (Jul 1-Sep 30) from DOR's dealer rate flyer ("combined" column + 0.5% MV tax):** Seattle **11.05%** (10.55 + 0.5) -- so 10.35% is wrong/low for a Seattle-sourced deal; Bellevue / Kirkland / Bothell (King) / unincorporated King (RTA) 10.80%; Renton, Auburn (King), Tacoma 11.00%; Lynnwood 11.20%; Everett 10.40%; Puyallup 10.80%; Fife 10.70%; Olympia 10.20%; Burlington 9.10%; Bellingham 9.40%. Practical upshot: the same $26,000 car costs ~$500 less in tax bought from a Burlington/Bellingham-area dealer than from a Seattle one, and an Oregon purchase is taxed at your Seattle use-tax rate (11.05%). Ask each WA dealer to state the rate/location code they applied. (Rates re-verify each quarter; Q4 flyer posts ~late Aug/Sep.) [V] -- https://dor.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/Q326_MVET-flyer-by-county.pdf ; https://dor.wa.gov/taxes-rates/sales-use-tax-rates/local-sales-use-tax/local-sales-use-tax-rate-history-motor-vehicle-sales-or-leases
- **Buying from an Oregon dealer as a WA resident.** Oregon has no sales tax, but WA use tax is owed on the purchase price at the rate for your residence (Seattle 11.05% in Q3 2026), with credit only for sales tax actually paid elsewhere (none in OR); it is due when the vehicle is titled/registered in WA. Many Portland-metro franchise dealers are set up to collect WA use tax and process WA title/plates for you (Vancouver/Portland groups describe doing exactly this), but that is dealer-by-dealer -- ask in the first email: "Do you collect Washington use tax and file WA title/registration, or do I take an Oregon trip permit and pay at a King County licensing office?" If they do not, you drive home on an Oregon light-vehicle trip permit ($35, 21 days) and pay use tax + fees at DOL; note DOL can substitute fair-market value if a private-sale price looks low, but a dealer bill of sale is normally accepted. Note DOL's own use-tax page still shows the old 0.3% figure; DOR's 0.5% (2026) controls. - **Oregon doc fee cap: $250 if the dealer uses an "integrator" (electronic filing vendor), $200 if not; buyer may negotiate it (ORS 822.043; Oregon DOJ).** Oregon's 0.5% "vehicle privilege/use tax" applies to *new* vehicles only, so it should not appear on a used CX-5; a "CAT"/"corporate activity tax recovery" line is a dealer pass-through, not a government tax -- negotiable. [V] -- https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_822.043 ; https://www.doj.state.or.us/consumer-protection/motor-vehicles/buying-a-vehicle/ ; https://www.oregon.gov/dor/programs/businesses/Pages/Vehicle-privilege-and-use-taxes.aspx ; https://www.oregon.gov/dor/programs/businesses/pages/corporate-activity-tax.aspx
- **WA title/registration ballpark for a ~$26k used CX-5 garaged in Seattle (separate from sales/use tax): roughly $500-$750 in year one, of which the Sound Transit RTA excise tax is the surprise item.** RTA MVET = 1.1% x (original MSRP x statutory depreciation %), where years of service = current year - model year + 1; DOL's schedule: yr 4 = 83%, yr 5 = 74%, yr 6 = 65%. With CX-5 MSRPs of ~$27k (S) to ~$38-40k (Turbo/Signature): a 2023 (yr 4) runs ~$250-$350, a 2022 (yr 5) ~$220-$310, a 2021 (yr 6) ~$195-$275 -- and it recurs every year at tab renewal, declining with the schedule. It is based on MSRP, not what you paid, so a high-trim Signature costs more in RTA than a base S at the same purchase price. On top of RTA, expect fixed state/local items of very roughly $200-$350: $30 basic license + $35 weight fee (4,000-lb class, 2026 amounts) + Seattle TBD $50 + title/registration filing and service fees (raised 1/1/2026: e.g., title service $18, filing $6.50/$6.00, registration service $11) + ~$50 for new WA plates if the car does not already carry them (an Oregon car will not). Use DOL's fee calculator with the actual VIN for the exact figure; dealers that do WA licensing will estimate this line and true it up.
